Battlefield terrain & sources

These maps combine measured elevation with a reconstruction of the historical landscape. The hills and valleys come from digital elevation data. Woodland edges, fields, paths, watercourses and building symbols are interpreted from historical references; their exact outlines are not surveys of the battle date. Sector boundaries follow terrain features but are designed for gameplay.

Elevation and cartography

Terrain is derived from Mapzen Terrain Tiles, reprojected to the battlefield frame and rendered with shaded relief. Contours show elevations in metres above mean sea level (negative around Lake Tiberias). Lighting is exaggerated to make low ridges legible. A scale bar indicates horizontal distances.

  • Europe terrain: produced using Copernicus data and information funded by the European Union — EU-DEM layers.
  • United Kingdom terrain: © Environment Agency copyright and/or database right 2015. All rights reserved. Open Government Licence v3.
  • NED, SRTM and GMTED2010 terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey. Derived data have been resampled, smoothed, shaded and styled for this game.

The source baseline is modern. At Waterloo we interpolate over the post-battle Lion’s Mound; other landscape alterations can remain. At Hattin, invalid elevation samples are replaced from neighbouring valid samples, and the modern lake surface gives an approximate shoreline, not its precise level in 1187. Modern streets, railways and housing estates are not added to the historical artwork.

Waterloo · 1815

The ridge, farm strongpoints and approaches follow the National Army Museum account and Waterloo Association battle description. Hougoumont’s wood and orchard are reconstructed rather than copied from present-day land cover. Field enclosures are illustrative.

Sekigahara · 1600

Encampments and the valley setting follow the Gifu battlefield guide and National Park Service / Gifu museum account. Corrected landmark coordinates use SekigaharaLocationData, 株式会社CCL 大垣支社, licensed under CC BY 3.0. The forest extent is reconstructed, not a record of individual trees in 1600.

Hastings · 1066

The traditional Battle Abbey location and southeast approach follow English Heritage’s landscape assessment and Historic England. The precise extent, brook courses and orientation of the fighting remain uncertain. No post-battle abbey is depicted.

Hattin · 1187

The final approach, hills and importance of water follow Fordham’s translated contemporary account and Britannica. Springs, paths, vegetation and drainage are reconstructions; no claim is made that the modern shoreline matches July 1187 exactly.

Gettysburg · 1863

The July 2–3 battlefield follows the National Park Service landscape guide and battle overview. Elevation comes from USGS National Elevation Dataset tiles (Pennsylvania South 2007) through Mapzen. Woods, orchards, Plum Run, Rock Creek and roads are approximate historical reconstructions. Fields are illustrative; modern monuments and roads are not added. The opening deployment and sector edges are gameplay approximations, and artillery, cavalry and timed arrivals are not separately simulated.

Map artwork is generated and bundled with BorderStrife. No external map requests are made during play. The repository contains the clipped elevation rasters, source tile URLs and checksums, and reproducible build scripts.
